I played Resident Evil 4 on PS2, it was awesome, but not enough to get to the top 25. I've tried playing the Silent Hills (on the PSP) and wuss out, every time. I couldn't even get through the demo of Resident Evil Requiem on the Switch 2. 

## 15. Baldurs Gate 3

![Baldur's Gate 3 key art, via baldursgate3.game](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/baldurs-gate-3.webp)

My first and only playthrough took 140 hours, but damn what a game. It's a tremendous story, with tremendous characters, lore and quest-lines. As my first foray into Dungeons & Dragons in general, it introduced me to an entire world in such a compelling, awesome way. The gameplay took a second to grasp, but I eventually found a set of skills/spells that let me work through the main story - looking back, I now see that there are a dozen different ways I could have gone through things. I played this game on my Macbook while digital nomading / slowmading with my wife, and it proved to me that Mac as a gaming platform can succeed - it just needs Apple to make the Game Developer Toolkit to run natively, as Steam runs Proton on Linux, since we cannot depend on publishers to do the 'right thing' and make things multi-platform by default. If all game developers worked like Larian, then the world would be a better place.

## 14. Wii Sports

![Wii Sports, via nintendo.com](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/wii-sports.webp)

The game that singlehandedly convince my mom to buy me a Wii. While the Wii itself was a bit of a dissapointment to me outside of Twilight Princess, I spent hours playing Tennis, Bowling and Baseball by myself or with friends. A couple years back, when I discovered golf, I dug out my old Wii out of storage, bought Wii Sports resort, and had a blast playing it with the Wii Motion Plus. While Wii Sports, and the Wii in general, hasn't really aged that well, it is historic for the fact that it introduced millions to videogames in a way that they hadn't before and that it was bundled with the system. In todays day and age, that makes it all the greater.

## 12. Yugioh Eternal Duelist Soul

![Yu-Gi-Oh! Early Days Collection, which includes The Eternal Duelist Soul, via yugioh-card.com](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/yugioh-early-days-collection.webp)

I love Yugioh, and if you don't then you will probably say 'gtfo, how is this better than X, which isn't even on this list'. Well, it's my top 25. As a kid who grew up watching the original Saturday morning Yugioh cartoons on the WB, playing the Eternal Duelist Soul let me play the game on the go, and occasionally with someone else who happened to have the game too. It's hard to understand for someone who doesn't know how to play Yugioh, or doesn't appreciate it, but there the game had this sweet spot where the cards were simple, gameplay was fast and competitive, unlocking a good card was an exciting moment, and the characters in the game (sort of) used the cards they would use in the anime. As a handheld game, it was something you could pick up, play, and put down - something that is dying in today's world where everything is a Steam Deck, or a massive 8' Nintendo Switch 2 that isn't really portable at all. I recently replayed this game in the recently released 'Early Days' collection, and as a simple introduction to Yugioh, it's still functional - even if getting hit by a Raigeki feels cheap, even if everyone has a copy of Mirror Force in the deck, even if the enemies don't even have all the cards their deck SHOULD have because of the tiny card pool, it's still fun to play. 

## 11. Fallout 3 / New Vegas / 4

![Fallout 3 key art, via fallout.bethesda.net](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/fallout-3.webp)

I put these all in the same category, especially playing without mods; Fallout 3 was the most 'amazing' out of all of them, because playing that in 2008 was a mind-blowing experience, despite the primitive gunplay and forgettable story. Fallout New Vegas had the best setting, factions & writing, but it also had the most bugs & instability. Fallout 4, in Hardore Survival mode, for the first 20-30 hours or so is the most satisfying gameplay the series has ever offered (and is the only way I have ever played the game). They all have their strengths, they all have their worts - I don't particularly feel like ranking one above the other (though if you had to put a gun to my head, I would say Fallout 3).

## 10. Tony Hawks Pro Skater 3 - Tony Hawks Underground 2: Remix 

![Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 3 + 4 key art, via tonyhawkthegame.com](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/tony-hawks-pro-skater-3-4.webp)

There was a time in my life, with a PS2 at my command, that I played the hell out of all the Tony Hawk games. THPS3 was the gateway drug - I spent hours and hours and hours grinding, flipping & Christ Air-ing all over the place. And, in my opinion, it has the best licensed soundtrack of any game - ever. Full stop. While I played Pro Skater 4, Underground 1, and American Wasteland, Underground 2: Remix on the PSP became a permanent fixture on the road. The story was hilarious, and I'm sure if I replayed it now 15 years later, I would understand and appreciate even more of the humor with the benefit of age and urban dictionary. I must have replayed it at least 3 times. An honorable mention goes out to the DS version of American Sk8land - a great game in it's own right, a fully immersive 3D skating game (essentially at launch) that has the honor of being such a demanding game to run, that it is one of 2 or 3 games that doesn't run smoothly on an R4 card if you try to emulate it.

## 9. Red Dead Redemption 2

![Red Dead Redemption 2 key art, via rockstargames.com](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/red-dead-redemption-2.webp)

One of the best stories in games, one of the best main characters in games, one of the most realized interactive 'communities' in games, and almost certainly the best 'open world' in games, though to be fair I haven't played GTA 6 yet. I played it in 3rd person mode on Google Stadia during the pandemic, and then replayed it in first-person mode on PC - which somehow made the experience even better. The world is utterly massive, and it's easy to get lost in just exploring, hunting, and taking in the sights. A true masterpiece. 

## 8. Cyberpunk 2077

![Cyberpunk 2077 key art, via cyberpunk.net](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/cyberpunk-2077.webp)

In 2020, I was so damn hyped up for this game. I hadn't been hyped up for a game like this since Skyrim, back when it launched on 11.11.11 my freshman year of college. When CP got it's release date announced, I didn't actually have a game console or a PC that could hang - it was with great surprise that I saw it would launch on Google Stadia, at launch, for the same price. After asking myself 'what the hell is Google Stadia', I saw that it was essentially a Chromecast that could stream games, and that I could buy a 'founders edition' for something like $50 or $60 that included the Chromecast AND a modern game controller, maybe 6 months before release. I saw that it's library had RDR2, so that occupied my time until launch day, when I sunk in and THOROUGHLY enjoyed streaming the PC edition of Cyberpunk for 60+ hours while everyone with a gaming console burst a blood vessel raging about how horrible the performance was. A year or so later, Google Stadia shut down, and they refunded me all the money I paid for all the games on there, and the $50 for the Chromecast and Controller, so essentially I got to play the launch edition game for free. Years later, when I bought a Steam Deck, I jumped back in to play the game with the 2.0 update and Phantom Liberty, this time with Chinese voice acting so I could improve my language skills. The game was even better with the 2.0 update, and the Chinese localization was amazing, helping everything feel beyond brand new. 

Not to mention, the game is pretty damn awesome; the gunplay, the characters, the story, and Night City itself. While I would say the interactivity of the open world was ultimately a bit dissapointing, I would say it is less a problem of the creators vision, and more a function of the limitations of the then current generation of technology. 

## 7. Metal Gear Solid 3: Snake Eater

![Metal Gear Solid: Master Collection Vol. 1, featuring Snake Eater, via konami.com](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/metal-gear-solid-master-collection.webp)

One of the best, if not the best, standalone stories in all of videogames. Introduces a cast of truly iconic characters such as Naked Snake & the Boss, & while providing a once-in-a-generation action game experience that tells a story that appeals to the political scientist in me. I've played it on the PS2, the PS Vita, and on the Nintendo 3DS - at some point, I will carve out time to play the remake that came out recently.

## 6. Grand Theft Auto 3

![Grand Theft Auto III cover art, via rockstargames.com](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/grand-theft-auto-3.webp)

An affordable $4.99 used at Gamestop, this game was the first open-world game I ever played, and I spent dozens of hours outside the main story just farting around. Climbing up the stairs behind Luigi's club, mowing down the local population until a six star wanted level was achieved, hopping into a pre-parked Banshee below, and seeing how long I could last. I LARPed as a taxi driver, a paramedic and a police officer to see how high the score could go up. I never ended up collecting many of the 'secret packages' (I've never been much of a completionist) but GTA3 is what introduced me to the world of humor and comedy in a way that I never understood before with Chatterbox Radio. Radio DJ Laslow's show lasted about 50 minutes, and the deep, biting sarcasm was a revelation for a young person whose only exposure to comedy at that point was syndicated sitcoms like Friends, Seinfeld, or random programming caught on the WB like Reba. Throughout all of the GTA games since then, listening to the public radio stations has been a hoot, and one of my absolute favorite things to do.

## 5. Breath of the Wild

![The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild key art, via nintendo.com](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/breath-of-the-wild.webp)

I didn't quite 'get it' at first, when I played Breath of the Wild for the first time. I had played Twilight Princess, which I kind of enjoyed as my first Zelda game, and I really enjoyed the Ocarina of Time on the 3DS, and I suppose I enjoyed my first playthrough of Breath of the Wild on Switch as well. I bought the DLC for the game after completing it, and decided to try Master Mode, and from here I sunk probably 200 hours exploring the world, finding Koroks, discovering all the Shrines, finishing every quest, until I thoroughly finished this masterpiece of a game. 

## 4. Skyrim

![Skyrim Anniversary Edition art, via elderscrolls.bethesda.net](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/skyrim.webp)

On November 11th, 2011 in my freshman year of college, I stood outside a Gamestop at midnight for the first time ever, to buy a game on launch night. That night, I probably played it for 3 hours or so, before being too exhausted to continue, and over the next couple of months played it enough to finish the story - I think. To be honest, I don't even remember if I finished the game in that playthrough - it was college, an exciting time, and there were far more interesting things to do than play videogames, and I essentially took a break from them completely between then and 2015. At which point, while visiting my cousins in California while interning for Congressman Mike Honda, I borrowed my cousin's Switch to play Skyrim and proceeded to put 100 hours into a full playthrough of the game, as if for the first time. 

As someone who grew up playing handheld video games, it was shocking that the entire game fit on the Switch, with seemingly zero compromises or performance issues in my experience. All the voice acting, all the DLC - then, years later when I got my own Nintendo Switch, I put in ANOTHER 100 hours into a new playthrough - that's the kind of game Skyrim is. Accessible, fun, captivating, difficult to put down, yet easy to put away and come back to. The ambiance, the world building, the combat - a truly iconic game, and one that inspired a generation of gamers to become content creators. A year ago or so, I happened to inherit a Switch from a friend (shoutout to Steven Povlitz, you are the best dude) and realized something called 'The Anniversary Edition' existed, which added a bunch of official mods and a 'Survivor' mode to the game, similar to FO4's. When I got a Switch 2, I was happy to find out that there was a free Next Gen Update available for the game, with the save data importing flawlessly, to allow me to start a new run all over again. It's the kind of game people will be playing until the heat death of the universe.

## 3. Pokemon Red & Blue - Gen 2 Crystal

![Pokémon Red & Blue box art, via pokemon.com](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/pokemon-red-blue.webp)

When I first moved to America at 4 and a half years old, I didn't speak a lick of English. While I don't remember this happening, my mom told me that, at the first daycare I ever went to, a bunch of kids circled around me and yelled 'monster, monster' over my lack of language skills. This was at De Vargas Elementary, in San Jose California where there are people with all sorts of cultures and immigrant backgrounds, so I'm pretty sure we were playing a game of some sort, instead of subjecting me to some form of multiplayer bullying. At this time, in 1999, Pokemon mania was sweeping the after-school daycare. Every day after school, kids between kindergarten and 6th grade would whip out their GameBoys and GameBoy Colors and play Pokemon Red or Blue, trade and battle each other. All I could do was watch other people play, because I didn't have a GameBoy of my own, but I essentially knew the entire map of Kanto by heart without every playing it! I can sympathise with the kids who watch streamers today, I suppose. On my 6th birthday, my mom bought me a GameBoy Color, blue, along with a couple of games that did not include Pokemon. I probably raised up a stink, because a few weeks later we walked into the Toys R Us on Winchester Boulevard, just south of the 280 freeway where Santana Row was just starting construction. I don't remember if I wanted Pokemon Red, and they just didn't have it, but I walked out with a copy of Pokemon Blue. (side note: always been drawn to the Democratic Party, not sure why). Armed with my knowledge of Pokemon names, due to the Pokemon rap at the end of the TV show, and my kindergarten level literacy, I solidified my grasp of the English language and reading by curb-stomping any Pokemon trainer that came my way. Pokemon taught me how to read in English. 

## 2. The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt

![The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt key art, via thewitcher.com](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/witcher-3.webp)

This game may be a bit too high on this list, but as someone who appreciates a good yarn along with good gameplay, what really takes it over the top is CD Projekt Red's commitment to world-class localization. Coming from a family of Russian speakers, it is easy to never develop Russian language skills while living in a country that doesn't have opportunities to practice it. Due to my mom sending me to Russian school on Saturdays as a kid, I learned to how to read and write in the Cyrillic alphabet, but as an adult never had an opportunity to practice those skills nor learn any vocabulary outside of what a 11 year old boy might know. The Witcher 3, with it's perfect Russian audio and interface localization, provided the perfect springboard for me to learn a lot of new words, expressions and cultural knowledge in a way that didn't feel like work or school. To be clear, the first time I played the Witcher 3, it was on the Switch and it was in English. I remember that, at the time, I didn't have a Switch, so I ordered a Switch Lite and a copy of the newly launched Witcher 3 port (on launch day) and played through the base game and both DLCs in English on that infernal, tiny, low resolution screen. After that playthrough, I learned that it had full Russian localization, so I started another playthrough (which went maybe 40 hours) before I upgraded to the PC version on a Steam Deck where I started a fresh playthrough again, this time in Russian again. All the while, the gameplay and world building remained fresh, and the character of Geralt is in a level of iconic-ness right next to Naked Snake in my video game character Mount Rushmore.

## 1. Morrowind

![Morrowind gameplay screenshot, via elderscrolls.bethesda.net](/blog-assets/top-ten-videogames-of-all-time/morrowind.webp)

Playing this on a piece of trash 2007-era Intel Atom netbook with an 11.6 inch screen, at the lowest possible graphics settings, with so much fog in the distance you couldn't see ANYTHING, was the best gaming experience of my life. The world was massive, full of mystery and adventure; there were guilds of warriors, mages, thieves, killers & more. You could get involved with the geopolitics of the area, become a werewolf, make your own adventure, or actually do the story quests. One of my favorite parts of the game is the lack of a minimap, the development of which in Oblivion has been a curse upon the videogame world, as it encouraged not just you the player to follow written instruction (follow this path, turn right, go to this cave) but also the developers themselves to think carefully about where they place buildings, markers, and more with a level of attention and detail that gets lost when they have the luxury of thinking 'well, the minimap will tell them where to go, so who cares'. The minimap has made developers lazy, and it has made the players lazy, and it's a god damn tragedy. The lack of fast travel outside of in-universe methods (silt striders, teleporting between mage guilds) also encouraged you not just to explore the world, but to engage with crafting items, such as the boots of blinding speed that made you a) fast and b) blind. Even playing on a machine that could only experience the game on the most primitive of settings, my imagination did the rest, and I've never been sucked into a world quite like Morrowind's.
